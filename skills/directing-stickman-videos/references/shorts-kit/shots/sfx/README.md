# Sound effects: how to use them

Rules live in [§8 of `docs/soul/06-audio.md`](../../soul/06-audio.md). This page is the how-to: the bundled pack, the
code, and worked cases. The practice comes from one channel that put 17 effects on 8 videos and learned where they
help and where they hurt.

## The idea in one line

The video is a dry, credible explainer. An effect is the exclamation mark the **situation already earned**: it says
out loud what the viewer is already thinking. It never cheers a flat moment up.

## The bundled pack (CC0)

`public/sfx/` ships eight effects generated by the kit itself with ffmpeg, so there is no third-party audio and no
licence to track. Each long effect is normalised to about −20 LUFS. The two short ones (`pop`, `tick`) are too short
for a loudness measurement, so they were matched by peak.

| id | Use it when | Do not use it for | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| [`boom`](boom.md) | the turn: the fact that flips the story | the hook's first second, before anything is at stake | 1.65 s |
| [`ding`](ding.md) | the answer lands, the payoff | ordinary facts | 1.65 s |
| [`drop`](drop.md) | a plan fails, a hope is refused | deaths and disasters (see the tone rule in README) | 0.5 s |
| [`glitch`](glitch.md) | something is wrong: a myth, a broken record, a map error | a real tragedy | 0.65 s |
| [`pop`](pop.md) | a number, label or chip appears on screen | words that are only spoken | 0.23 s |
| [`riser`](riser.md) | the half second before a reveal | anything that is not followed by a reveal | 2.05 s |
| [`tick`](tick.md) | counting up or down on screen (days, years, items) | background rhythm | 0.11 s |
| [`whoosh`](whoosh.md) | the camera or the story jumps to a new place or time | every scene change (it becomes a metronome) | 0.85 s |

Each effect is a card here (`<id>.md`) whose front matter is the one place its file, length, gain, loudness,
licence, use and pairings are written. `bun run check` fails when `src/shared/audio/Sfx.tsx` disagrees with a card,
so the code, this table and the cards cannot drift apart. A file of your own (`src` in a cue) gets a card too:
copy one, set `file`, `loudness` and `licence`.

## How to cue an effect

Anchor every cue to a **word in the narration**, never to a frame number you typed.

```tsx
// src/videos/<id>/Video.tsx
import voice from "./voice.json";
import {FPS} from "../../shared/theme";
import {frameAtWord} from "../../shared/timings";
import {ShortsTemplate} from "../../shared/frame/ShortsTemplate";

const cue = (scene: string, word: string, nth?: number) => frameAtWord(voice, FPS, scene, word, nth);

const SFX_CUES = [
  {at: cue("turn", "never"), id: "boom"},
  {at: cue("payoff", "blue"), id: "ding"},
] as const;

export const Video = () => <ShortsTemplate voice={voice} /* … */ sfx={SFX_CUES} />;
```

Why words and not frames: the first version of this channel typed frame numbers into the cue list. It worked
exactly once. Changing the TTS engine moved every word by 10–30%, three effects landed in the middle of unrelated
words, and nothing reported an error: it just sounded wrong. With word anchors, `bun run tts <id>` re-times the
voice and every effect moves with it. If a word disappears from the script, `frameAtWord` throws at build time,
which is where you want to find out.

`nth` picks the second or third time a word appears in that scene (default 1). Matching ignores case and
punctuation.

### Your own files

```tsx
{at: cue("hook", "zero"), src: "sfx/my-airhorn.mp3", volume: 0.5}
```

Before using a new file: measure it and set its gain so it lands near −20 LUFS:

```bash
ffmpeg -hide_banner -i public/sfx/my-airhorn.mp3 -af ebur128=framelog=quiet -f null -
# gain = 10^((-20 - I) / 20)   where I is the "I:" value printed
```

The first seven files on the original channel ranged from −6.5 to −17.8 LUFS, a spread of 11.3 dB. With one shared
volume, the loud one hurt and the quiet one vanished. Then record the file's licence in `docs/facts/sources.json`
(`"kind": "sfx"`).

## Placement rules, with the cases that taught them

1. **Four or five per video at most** (`sfxMaxPerVideo`). On a 60–90 s video, two or three is usually right.
   Spread evenly, effects turn an explainer into a meme video and spend the authority the serious parts built.
   One video built five cues and then cut two: both sat just before a punch that was already there (one 30 frames
   before the strong word). The three that stayed hit harder.
2. **Put it where the situation is funny or sharp, not where the video is slow.** Good cases from the original
   channel: a king refuses to sell his horses, and the "get out" sound lands on "refused"; ninety thousand soldiers
   cross a desert to fetch horses, and the explosion lands after the number. Both facts were already absurd. The
   effect only said so.
3. **Never on top of a sentence.** Cue the word where a sentence *ends* or the first word after a pause, so the
   viewer hears both. Do not stack an effect on the strong word or on an exclamation. Those need a beat of silence
   to land, so put the effect before them, not on them.
4. **Tone.** When the fact is a death toll or a disaster, use no effect, or at most a quiet `boom`/`drop` placed
   after the number, never a comic sound. A comic sound on suffering reads as mockery, and the comments will say so.
5. **Level.** All effects play at `SFX_VOLUME = 0.7` × the file's gain, which is about 3 dB under the voice. That
   number came from listening. Level with the voice was right on paper, but in the real render the effect jumped
   out of the narration and pulled the whole frame toward a meme video. Do not go below 0.5: under that, it sounds
   like a mixing mistake rather than a joke.
6. **Cues live at the composition root**, not inside a scene's `<Sequence>`. An effect often falls on a scene
   boundary, and inside a scene it would be cut off at the cut. `ShortsTemplate` does this for you.
7. **Duration follows the real fps.** Each effect declares `seconds` and the player converts with the composition's
   fps. The original code hard-coded 30 fps, and when one video moved to 60 fps every effect was silently cut in
   half.

## Meme sounds

Popular meme sounds (the Roblox "oof", the Among Us kill, famous TV and game clips) are **owned audio**. They are
everywhere on TikTok and almost nobody gets punished, but that is not a licence. Content ID can match them, and they
must **never be committed to a public repository**. If your channel decides to use them anyway, keep them out of git
(add the files to `.gitignore`), write the risk into your own credits file, and know that you can replace them later
by swapping the file and its gain, with no scene changes.

Licensed alternatives: Pixabay Sound Effects, freesound.org filtered to CC0, or your own recordings.

## Checklist

- [ ] ≤ `sfxMaxPerVideo` cues, and each one is on a moment the facts already made funny or sharp
- [ ] Every cue uses `frameAtWord`, with no typed frame numbers
- [ ] No cue sits on top of a sentence, the strong word or a death toll
- [ ] Every custom file is measured, has a gain, and has a licence record
- [ ] Listened to the render once with effects and once muted: the video still works without them

For a promo (a film led by picture and music rather than voice): the method, the five sound rules
and how to source and pin third-party effects are in [`docs/promo/sound.md`](../../promo/sound.md).
